Heritage

Conserving buildings of character. We are firm believers that timely intervention and regular maintenance are key to sustaining the value of a listed building, and we bring a deep understanding of traditional materials and construction to every historic project.

What we do

Historic buildings need a different approach: traditional methods, sympathetic techniques and a working knowledge of the planning framework that protects them. We have long-standing relationships with conservation specialists and local authority officers, and consult archaeologists and other specialists where a project needs them.

  • Listed Building Repairs

    Assessing issues and specifying repairs using traditional methods and sympathetic techniques, drawing on our relationships with conservation craftspeople and local authority officers.

  • Maintenance Programmes and Investigative Reports

    Forecasting budgets, producing schedules of work for tendering, liaising with conservation officers and overseeing the works, so the upkeep of a heritage asset is planned rather than reactive.

  • Historic Building Recording

    Listed Building Consent may require an Historic Building Record before and during works. We produce records from Level 1 (photographs and notes) to Level 4 (comprehensive analytical record), in line with Historic England guidance.

  • Heritage Statements

    Researched statements for Listed Building Consent applications, describing the significance of the building and the impact of the proposals using photographs, historical maps, sketches and on-site analysis.

  • Quinquennial Inspections

    Every five years, church buildings should be inspected by a suitably experienced and qualified professional. We are approved by the Dioceses of St Albans and Oxford and currently look after thirteen churches, from medieval to modern.

  • Church Restoration

    Beyond the quinquennial inspection, we advise churches on re-ordering and long-term projects, with expertise grounded in traditional materials and construction techniques.

Repair, not replace.

Our starting point is always to retain as much historic fabric as possible. Where change is needed, we aim for work that is honest, reversible where it can be, and of a quality that will itself be valued in a hundred years. The practice holds the RICS Conservation accreditation and is a member of the SPAB.
